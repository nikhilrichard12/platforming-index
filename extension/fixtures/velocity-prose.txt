Outdoor battery storage, 30 kW class: what the build usually looks like

For a 30 kW outdoor battery cabinet the thermal path tends to drive the rest
of the design. Most integrators start with forced air, so a 120 mm axial fan
running on 24 VDC, a wire fan guard over it, and an extruded aluminum heatsink
around 200 mm on the hottest devices. Where ambient runs above 45 C a liquid
cooling cold plate is the usual answer, though it adds a service burden.

The power stage is typically built from half bridge IGBT modules rated
1200 V 100 A, with an electrolytic DC link capacitor of 470 uF 450 V across
the bus and an isolated gate driver board, 6 channel, per leg. Control sits on
a DSP control board, with an NTC temperature sensor 10k at four points and a
hall effect current sensor 100 A per phase. On the DC side you will also need
a DC contactor 150 A, a copper busbar 200 A, and a 6 pole terminal block 30 A
for the auxiliary wiring.

Commercially, the segment grew 14 percent last year and the main constraint is
installer availability rather than component supply.
