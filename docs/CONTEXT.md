# Context: why this product, and how it gets judged

## The problem, from Kearney's own research

Kearney PERLab surveyed 100 senior leaders at manufacturers with $1B+ revenue (article: "The current state of platforming and the hidden gap", Nov 4, 2026). Findings:

- Most leaders know platforming. Far fewer run it at scale.
- Even companies that claim scale mostly share parts. Few built a common core with plug-in modules.
- Three blockers: engineering effort (43% name it the top challenge), ownership split across functions with little C-suite review, and missing incentives (52% of at-scale companies have no complexity targets).
- Kearney's path: cost-out on current products first, then consolidate duplicate parts and variants, then platforms and modules. Early savings fund later steps.
- Metrics Kearney wants tracked: reuse rate, approved module adoption, part-number reduction, working-capital release, time to market.

Two of the article's authors, Alex Gallard and Andrew Cecola, are mentors at this hackathon.

## What a PERLab mentor told us (Nov 13)

- The hard part of platforming is not building it. Companies do not want to do it. They do not understand it and do not see the value against the effort.
- You cannot platform existing products retroactively.
- Kearney builds client dashboards with platforming measures inside and gradually shows impact in metrics the client cares about: time to market, cost, complexity.
- Wanted: something that shows every function (sales, marketing, CEO, finance) the impact in its own terms.
- Kearney is starting a "platforming index": a survey that sizes up a prospect's complexity for business development.

## Track 1 brief

"Build a tool that identifies platforming opportunities across a manufacturer's existing products, finding assemblies and components that can be standardized to reduce complexity. It should also help companies determine how to build a common core platform plus add-on modules that still meet all their customers' needs."

The matcher and the first-cut common core cover the brief. The index, agent, and procurement hook cover the adoption problem.

## How Kearney would use it

- Before a client signs: score a prospect, send the survey, present the index.
- During a project: generate the dashboard consultants now build by hand.
- After: the procurement hook keeps savings from eroding, and the index shows when the client is ready for the next step.

## What we have that Kearney does not

- Value shown before the first contract.
- A dashboard that explains itself differently to each role.
- A view of where functions disagree.
- A check at the moment someone adds a new part.

## Rubric (each scored 1 to 5)

- Commercial potential, 25%: clear target customer, demand evidence, competitive advantage, business model with pricing and costs, growth path.
- Technical feasibility, 25%: sound solution with justified tech choices, implementation plan with resources and costs, technical challenges addressed (integration, data accuracy, reliability, scale).
- Final pitch, 25%: 6 minutes plus 2 minutes Q&A. Clear problem, solution, advantage, impact, evidence for claims, awareness of limits.
- Deliverable, 15%: working prototype of core features, clear and well-designed.
- Research and use of Velocity AI, 10%: research that validates the problem and supports key decisions, and shows how it refined the solution. Some Velocity use is required and the pitch must mention it.
- Physical product: 3 bonus points. Not our focus.

Judges want to hear how we arrived at the solution, not only the result.

## Likely judge questions

- Why would a customer switch from the market leader?
- What stops a larger competitor from copying this?
- How do you get your first customers?
- Why this model, database, or framework?
- What happens at 100 times the scale?
- What is the hardest part of integrating with a company's existing systems?
- What is a weakness of the product and how do you manage it?
- If funded, what is the first thing you would do?
- What did Velocity AI help you refine?
- Why this track?

## Pitch line

Platforming fails in the boardroom, not the engineering lab. We built the tool that wins the boardroom, then keeps winning it every time someone buys a part.
